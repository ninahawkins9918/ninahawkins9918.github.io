Sumwright Privacy Policy

Effective October 30, 2026

Sumwright is a number puzzle for iPhone. Its App Store listing names Delaney Brooks (United States) as the party behind it, and the text below calls that party "the vendor". What follows covers what the app keeps, where it keeps it, and the one narrow case in which the vendor could end up with anything personal.

At a glance

  • Puzzles, progress and settings are saved inside the app on your iPhone and are not transmitted by it.
  • Sumwright never shows a permission request, has no sign-up, and contains nothing that measures, profiles or follows you from one app or website to another.
  • The vendor receives personal data only if you write to it by email.

1What the app saves on your iPhone

Everything below lives inside Sumwright's own sandbox on the device:

  • A grid in progress. Up to two of them: one free grid and one Today's grid. For each: the puzzle, the numbers you placed, the undo history, the time on its clock and the number of hints used.
  • Progress. For every size, difficulty and mode, the count of solved grids and the best time; the dates on which Today's grid was solved, which is where the streak comes from; and a short list of recently finished grids, so the same win is never counted twice.
  • Settings. Appearance, haptics, timer, equation highlighting, the last mode picked on Home, and whether the intro has been seen.
  • Set-aside files. When a saved file cannot be read, Sumwright renames it and starts clean instead of deleting it. Such a copy holds the same kind of game data and disappears when Sumwright is uninstalled.

A free grid is built from a random number drawn at the moment you start it; Today's grid is built from the calendar date on your iPhone. Neither number relates to you or to the device. The app keeps no device identifier, no name, no email address and no location.

Sumwright writes short technical notes to the iOS system log when a file cannot be saved or read (for example "session write failed"). These notes contain no puzzle content and stay on the device under iOS rules.

2Removing it

Sumwright has no reset button. Removing Sumwright from the iPhone wipes everything in section 1. Older iCloud or Mac/PC backups of your iPhone may still hold these files until that backup is replaced or deleted. Backups are controlled by you and Apple; the vendor has no access to them.

3Connections

There is no networking code in Sumwright, and apart from Apple's system frameworks every part of it was written for it. Nothing is uploaded, synced or shared by the app.

The About section in the app's Settings has two rows, Privacy Policy and Support. Each opens a page of this website in your browser. Like any website, the host of these pages receives technical request data such as your IP address and browser type, under the host's own terms. The pages set no cookies and load no counters, fonts or other outside content.

4Email to the vendor

Writing to nina.hawkins9918@gmail.com gives the vendor your address, your message and any attachment, together with what your mail program adds on its own, such as a display name. This mail is read to answer you and, where it describes a faulty grid or a bug, to repair it in a later version. Your mail is never sold or used to advertise, and no newsletter is ever sent to your address. A mail service carries and stores these messages for the vendor and may keep them on servers in another country. Correspondence is erased no later than twelve months after the last reply, and earlier on request.

5Data Apple handles

Apple operates the App Store and iOS and processes data about downloads, purchases on your account, ratings and device diagnostics as described at apple.com/legal/privacy. If the Share With App Developers switch is turned on (in iOS Settings: open Privacy & Security, scroll down to Analytics & Improvements), Apple may pass the vendor crash reports and aggregate usage numbers without your identity. The vendor looks at them only to find defects. Turning the switch off stops this.

6Children

The app's content is digits and the four arithmetic signs; it is rated 4+ and nothing a child does in it leaves the device. The vendor does not knowingly correspond with children under 13, or under the age of digital consent in their country. A parent who learns that a child has written may ask for that mail to be deleted.

7Your rights

Wherever you live, you may ask the vendor which personal data it has about you (in practice an email thread, or none). You may also ask for a copy of it, for corrections, for its deletion, or for its use to stop. Send the request to nina.hawkins9918@gmail.com; it is answered within one month and without a fee.

  • EEA, Switzerland and the UK. Handling your email rests on the vendor's legitimate interest in replying to it (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; the UK GDPR and the Swiss FADP set the same rule). You may also lodge a complaint with the data protection supervisor where you live, for example the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens in the Netherlands, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali in Italy, the authority of your federal state in Germany, or the ICO in Britain.
  • California (CCPA/CPRA). The vendor neither sells nor shares personal information as those laws define it, and using a right never changes how Sumwright works for you.
  • Other places. Local privacy law may add rights; the same address handles them.

8Changes

If Sumwright is ever changed to collect data, the change will be announced on this page, and the privacy label on its App Store listing will be corrected, before that version is out. Every revision changes the date at the top.
